3. THE ANSWERS
------------------------------------------------------------------------
Is the deleted pod's claim still allocated? No. The resourceclaim
controller (kube-controller-manager) clears reservedFor and the
allocation about 0.1-0.3 s after the pod is gone (the pod has no
nodeName yet, so its deletion is immediate), removes the
resource.kubernetes.io/delete-protection finalizer and the claim is
deleted within 0.4 s. No deletionTimestamp lingers, no finalizer of
ours (the pool controller and the driver add none to claims).

Is the scheduler still in the binding cycle of the deleted pod? Yes,
but only until the next poll of PreBind (0-5 s): it finds the claim
gone and fails; Unreserve drops the in-flight allocation. Not the
binding timeout (600 s): that applies only while the claim exists.

Is it the pool controller or the driver? No. The controller only acts
on claims with an allocation; the driver is not involved before the
pod is bound.

How long until the device is free? In the scheduler's books 0-5 s
after the delete. But a pod that was already pending on the device is
not retried when it gets free: it waits for the next queueing event
that the DynamicResources plugin turns into "Queue", or for the
UnschedulableTimeout flush, 5 min after its last failed attempt
(checked every 30 s: 5:00-5:30). Its last failed attempt is the
immediate retry right after the delete, so in practice 5:00-5:30 after
the delete (measured 5:23.6 and 5:05.8).

What frees it? The deletion noticed by the PreBind poll (claim not
found) frees the device; what lets the pending pod take it is a
later queueing event or the 5-minute flush. Not ctrl-start (the pool
slice is republished unchanged), not the binding timeout.

ctrl-start later: the controller reconciles the claims that have an
allocation of its pool. The deleted pod's claim no longer exists, so
nothing is attached for it, also when ctrl-start comes 0.34 s after
the delete (scenario B: the controller's first reconcile came 1.5 s
later, the claim was gone). Attachments whose owner claim is gone are
detached at its first reconcile (L2: a-pooled0@vm1 of claim 70664454,
deleted at 14:00:45 while the controller was down, detached at
14:11:11 when the controller came back). When the waiting pod finally
gets the device, the running controller attaches it within 1-3 s and
the pod binds.

4. WHY (SOURCES)
------------------------------------------------------------------------
DR/dynamicresources.go
- PreBind (1661-1780): bindClaim writes allocation and reservedFor;
  the in-flight allocation is removed only by prebindSuccessCleanup,
  which runs when PreBind returns success (1689-1693, 1850-1866;
  bindClaim 1805).
  Then it waits: wait.PollUntilContextTimeout(ctx, 5*time.Second,
  pl.bindingTimeout, true, isPodReadyForBinding) (1723).
- isPodReadyForBinding (2054-2081): gets each claim from the assume
  cache; a missing claim is an error, which ends the poll at once.
  A claim without allocation is "not ready" (isClaimReadyForBinding,
  1995) and isClaimTimeout needs an allocation (2030-2048), so a claim
  that still exists but was deallocated would be polled until the
  binding timeout (600 s, K/pkg/scheduler/apis/config/
  types_pluginargs.go:289). Not our case: our claims come from
  templates and are deleted with the pod.
- Unreserve (1582-1645): MaybeRemoveClaimPendingAllocation, then
  AssumedClaimRestore; the restore emits an event only for an object
  still in the cache (K/pkg/scheduler/util/assumecache/
  assume_cache.go:476-492); a deleted claim gives none.
- EventsToRegister (278-299): Node add/label/allocatable, ResourceClaim
  add/update/delete (with isSchedulableAfterClaimChange), the pod's
  generated claim, DeviceClass add/update, ResourceSlice add/update.
  No AssignedPodDelete.
- isSchedulableAfterClaimChange (379-470): any claim going from
  allocated to not allocated -> Queue (for every pending pod); a
  deleted claim without allocation -> QueueSkip; other claims'
  updates -> QueueSkip.
DR/dra_manager.go:321-345, 367-431: ListAllAllocatedDevices and
GatherAllocatedState count the in-flight allocations as allocated.
K/pkg/scheduler/eventhandlers.go deletePodFromSchedulingQueue (359)
and handleAssumedPodDeletion (268): a deleted assumed pod is removed
from the cache and AssignedPodDelete is sent; the binding goroutine is
not cancelled (the PreBind cancel of schedule_one.go:458-464 is used
only by preemption).
K/pkg/scheduler/schedule_one.go handleBindingCycleError (507): after
the failed PreBind, MoveAllToActiveOrBackoffQueue(AssignedPodDelete),
which DynamicResources does not listen to.
K/pkg/scheduler/backend/queue/scheduling_queue.go:65
DefaultPodMaxInUnschedulablePodsDuration = 5 min, flushed by
flushUnschedulableEntitiesLeftover every 30 s (507-515, 1306-1328).
K/pkg/features/kube_features.go:2119 SchedulerPopFromBackoffQ (beta,
on): the retry right after the delete runs at once, so it always
falls into the in-flight window and fails.
K/pkg/controller/resourceclaim/controller.go:1474-1709 syncClaim: a
deleted pod's reservation is dropped, the allocation is cleared when
reservedFor gets empty, the finalizer is removed.

In short: the release of an in-flight allocation by Unreserve, when
the claim was deleted first, produces no event the DRA plugin
listens to. That is a gap of kube-scheduler (worth an upstream issue:
"pods rejected by DynamicResources are not requeued when a deleted
pod's in-flight allocation is released in Unreserve"), not of ours.

5. WHAT THE MODEL GOT WRONG
------------------------------------------------------------------------
promote (model/actions.go:690-703) hands the device to its waiter as
soon as the holder is deleted, and the settle oracle demands the
waiter's claim allocated within the settle timeout (180 s). That holds
when the holder was bound (its claim's deallocation is the event that
requeues the waiter, and nothing is in flight), not when the holder's
pod was still in PreBind: phase attaching or starting with its pool
claim allocated and its binding conditions not yet true (controller
down, or the attach not yet done), i.e. the pod has no nodeName yet.

6. THE RULE FOR THE MODEL
------------------------------------------------------------------------
R1. Hold. When a pod is deleted while its claim is allocated and the
    scheduler waits for its binding conditions (the pod not bound
    yet), its device stays taken in the scheduler for up to 5 s after
    the delete (until the PreBind poll finds the claim gone). The
    claim itself is gone within 1 s; nothing is attached for it, also
    if ctrl-start comes right after.
R2. Stall. A pod that contends for that device and gets a scheduling
    attempt in that window stays Pending ("cannot allocate all
    claims", claim not allocated). That is every contender that was
    already Pending before the delete (the claim deallocation retries
    it at once, inside the window), and a contender created within 5 s
    after the delete.
R3. Release. A stalled contender gets the device (allocated, then
    waiting for the attach or attached) at the first of:
    a) any ResourceSlice add or update anywhere in the cluster (a
       node driver republishing its slice, e.g. after an attach or
       detach on any node or a kubelet restart that removed its
       slice; a ctrl-restart, which deletes and republishes the pool
       slices); NOT ctrl-start after ctrl-kill (unchanged slice);
    b) any claim in the cluster going from allocated to deallocated
       (the deletion of another pod whose claim was allocated) after
       the hold of R1 is over;
    c) a Node add, label or allocatable change, a DeviceClass change;
    d) otherwise 5 min to 5 min 30 s after the contender's last failed
       attempt, which is right after the delete.
    It cannot get it before the hold of R1 is over.

Oracle: the model cannot see a)-c) reliably, so in the window from
the delete until 330 s after it (take 360 s with margin) a stalled
contender is nondeterministic: accept both "Pending, claim not
allocated" and the promoted state (allocated, waiting for the attach,
or attached and starting when the controller is up). The device stays
contested meanwhile (no new creates for it, as now). After 360 s
without the promotion having been seen, require it. A settle in that
window must either not wait for the stalled contender (treat it as
settled in both states) or have a timeout of at least 360 s from the
delete; the first is cheaper, since otherwise every such step costs
5 min.

Simplest variant if the extra state is not wanted: when a holder in
PreBind is deleted and a contender is or becomes Pending within 5 s,
do not promote it; treat it as Pending until a ctrl-restart of its
pool, a kubelet-restart, or the 360 s are over, and accept the
promoted state at any time (a)-(c) happened behind the model's back).

Not chosen: making the executor nudge the scheduler (for instance a
label change on a node, which DynamicResources and NodeAffinity both
turn into "Queue") after such a delete. It would hide a delay that a
real user of the pool sees, and the test is about real behaviour.
