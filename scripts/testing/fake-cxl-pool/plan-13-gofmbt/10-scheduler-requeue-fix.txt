========================================
2026-10-27 (orchestrator). Inputs: bug-08-scheduler-no-requeue-after-
prebind-unreserve.txt, 08-analysis-claim-after-pod-deleted.txt. Code:
~/github.com/kubernetes/kubernetes, branch 5jr-dra-requeue (on
release-1.37, v1.37.1-21-gd8848b7f2a5; the cluster runs v1.37.1).
K = pkg/scheduler, DR = K/framework/plugins/dynamicresources.

1. The gap
----------
- DR keeps an allocation "in flight" from Reserve until PreBind
  succeeds (DR/dra_manager.go SignalClaimPendingAllocation,
  DR/dynamicresources.go bindClaim prebindSuccessCleanup). With binding
  conditions PreBind waits up to the binding timeout (600 s), polling
  every 5 s, so the in-flight allocation outlives the claim's
  deallocation and deletion by up to 5 s.
- When the pod is deleted, kube-controller-manager deallocates and
  deletes its claim at once. The deallocation (ResourceClaim update)
  requeues every pod that DR rejected (isSchedulableAfterClaimChange:
  allocated -> deallocated = Queue). They are retried at once and fail:
  the device is still in flight.
- PreBind's next poll finds the claim gone, PreBind fails, Unreserve
  drops the in-flight allocation, and handleBindingCycleError (K/
  schedule_one.go:507-534) moves pods with EventAssignedPodDelete:
  "'Forget'ing an assumed Pod in binding cycle should be treated as a
  PodDelete event, as the assumed Pod had occupied a certain amount of
  resources in scheduler cache."
- DR does not register AssignedPod/Delete (DR/dynamicresources.go
  EventsToRegister), so that event never requeues the pods it
  rejected. They wait for the 5 min unschedulable flush.

2. Options
----------
A Drop the in-flight allocation as soon as bindClaim has written the
  allocation and put it into the assume cache. The claim object then
  carries the allocation alone, and its deallocation frees the device
  before the requeue. Root cause, but it changes the life cycle of
  in-flight allocations that pod-group (gang) scheduling relies on
  (DR/dra_manager.go inFlightAllocationSharers comment), and the
  ordering between the informer update and the requeue would need its
  own proof. Larger review surface.
B Register AssignedPod/Delete in DR with a queueing hint: Queue when
  the deleted or forgotten pod had resource claims (spec.resourceClaims
  or a DRA extended resource claim in its status), QueueSkip otherwise.
  This is how NodeResourcesFit, NodePorts, the volume plugins and
  default preemption learn that an assumed pod's resources are free
  again, and it is the event the scheduler already sends after
  Unreserve in the binding cycle. The informer's event for the deletion
  itself gives one futile retry (the allocation is still in flight);
  the event after Unreserve gives the retry that succeeds.
C Make PreBind notice the claim's deletion at once (cancel the poll
  from the claim event handler). Shortens the window, still needs B to
  requeue.
D Let the executor or a user touch a node or slice to trigger a
  requeue: a workaround, not a fix.

3. Decision
-----------
B: small, follows the framework's own contract for assumed pods, needs
no change to in-flight bookkeeping, and also helps after a binding
timeout or a binding failure for any other reason. The scheduling
cycle's own Unreserve (Reserve or Permit failure) runs inside the one
scheduling cycle, so no other pod saw the in-flight allocation there.

Tests: a table test of the hint (deleted pod with claims, with an
extended resource claim, without claims, wrong object type), the
EventsToRegister expectation, and, if the plugin's tests allow it, a
scheduling test where a pod rejected by DR is retried after an
assumed pod with a claim is forgotten.

Verification in the cluster: deploy the fixed kube-scheduler on vm2
(static pod manifest, image built from the release-1.37 base with the
fixed binary), replay the D8 sequence (ctrl-kill a, create
xnuma-a@vm1#0, create xdax-a@vm1#0, delete xnuma-a@vm1#0, settle) and
measure the time from the delete to xdax-a's allocation: expected
within the backoff after the PreBind poll (~5-15 s), against 5:05 and
5:23 before. Then the model's A19 stall window can be shortened for a
fixed scheduler.
