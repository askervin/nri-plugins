F1  How gofmbt treats states today
----------------------------------
- State is an interface with String() only (G/state.go). The library
  never compares states except through String(); it never
  de-duplicates them while walking (01-findings-gofmbt G1).
- StateChange "must not modify the original state" (G/transition.go),
  but nothing says that the RETURNED state is final. After review
  finding F1 of plan-13 (05-review-GOFMBT.txt), the contract became:
  "States must not change while a method of the Coverer is running.
  Between the calls they may change: the Coverer does not remember
  their String()s from one call to the next" (G/cover.go Coverer
  doc, MarkCovered, BestPath, doc.go). The motivation was an on-line
  loop that updates its current state object in place with what it
  observed.
- The price of that contract is in G/cover.go: covCtx (buf, h, hist
  []stateStrs, rootIsHist, nodes, nodeStr, nodeOk, sameStart,
  roundStarted), stateStrs with startOk/endOk bits, sameState()
  (pointer identity through reflect), nodeString/startString/
  endString, and the init/place bookkeeping of a per-call memo: about
  130 lines whose only purpose is to call String() once per walked
  state within ONE call without trusting the state to stay the same
  across calls. Review F1 itself said the cross-call memo "saves at
  most maxWindow-1 String() calls per BestPath/MarkCovered, which is
  nothing next to b^d"; the same holds for most of the per-call memo
  if the model memoizes String() itself.
- Walkable.StepsFrom's result is shuffled in place by the search
  (G/search.go walk: c.shuffle(sr.m.StepsFrom(st))) and reordered by
  Walker's step filter; nothing documents who owns the slice. A
  Walkable that caches its steps must know.
- Actions: the toy and the player example create a new Action
  (Sprintf) on every generator call; the test90 model creates them
  once (M/planner.go newMbtModel). Not a contract, a cost.

F2  How the test90 model treats states
--------------------------------------
- Immutable by convention: every transition clones (M/state.go
  clone, copy-on-write slices for SlotData, LeaseIDs, UnblockAt, Ev);
  String() (the C4 abstraction) and the occupancy are memoized in the
  state with atomic.Pointer, "race-free: the planner and the oracle
  share states". Adoption of an observation returns a new state
  (M/oracle.go adoptDone, checkAlts; M/adopt.go applyAdoption clones).
  So the on-line loop never needed gofmbt's permission to mutate.
- The state carries fields that matter only to the protocol and the
  oracle, not to the search: Steps (the step number), Last (the
  action that led here; the C2 coverage function reads it instead of
  the step's action), HeldAt and UnblockAt (Unix times set by the
  oracle), Drv.Pod/PodBefore, LeaseIDs, SlotData and Data (tokens),
  Gen (pod name generation), Alts and Truncated (the other outcomes of
  a settle, for the oracle). Two states reached by different paths
  that differ only in these behave the same in next() and in the
  coverage functions.
- Fields that next() and the coverage functions read: Slots (Phase,
  Prev, Node, Restarts, RaceKill, Steer, Lease, LeaseMode, Slack),
  Ctrl (Up, Att, Slices), Drv (Restarts for max-restarts, Killed,
  Restarted, F, FMaybe), Stacks, Ev (order matters: issue order),
  Contested, Held, Released, Unsettled; cfg is shared.
- The planner (M/planner_ext.go) returns only the Act of path[0] (or
  path[1] in planAfter); no state of the search leaves the planner.
  The on-line loop computes the predicted state itself with
  from.apply(a) (M/online.go execute), and planAfter is rooted at the
  real from/to objects through forcedFirst (M/planner.go).

F4  De-duplication potential
----------------------------
Temporary test (full profile, on-line config, random walk of 48 steps
with seed 1, measured every 8 steps): the depth-d tree from the
current state, nodes = states created by StepsFrom, distinct = by the
full state dump without the step number.
  step  d  leaves   nodes    distinct states  distinct transitions
   0    3  261142   265317    92818 (1/2.9)   153204 (1/1.7)
   8    3  297874   302437    89771 (1/3.4)   163772 (1/1.8)
  16    3  285253   289682    67997 (1/4.3)   138991 (1/2.1)
  24    3  516552   523151   108077 (1/4.8)   238170 (1/2.2)
  32    3   21051    22162     8148 (1/2.7)    13110 (1/1.7)
  40    3   16667    17640     5821 (1/3.0)     9773 (1/1.8)
  48    3   94898    99126    20177 (1/4.9)    40360 (1/2.5)
  depth 2: distinct states 1/1.7-1/2.4 of nodes, transitions 1/1.1-1/1.3
  depth 1: 54-69 distinct of 64-81 (a few actions give equal states,
           e.g. read, and faults that change nothing yet)
So a cache of transitions by canonical state removes 40-60% of the
next() calls at depth 3 (the leaves' transitions are never computed:
a leaf is the END of a transition, the saving is on the internal
nodes' StepsFrom, i.e. on every state created). Since a leaf's
coverage strings depend on the path (C3 pairs, C2 on start state and
action), the walk itself cannot be shortened: the leaves are the
floor of the search, which is what the exponent means.
Across consecutive plans the trees overlap little: after executing
one step the new depth-3 tree shares b+b^2 of its b+b^2+b^3 nodes with
the old one (~3% at b=31). A cache per plan loses almost nothing; a
cache across plans would grow with the run.

F5  Memory
----------
50-60 MB RSS for everything (depth 3, 400 steps; depth 4 too). Each
State is ~250 bytes plus 43 SlotStates (12 bytes each) plus shared
slices; a depth-3 tree of ~100k distinct states is ~100 MB if kept.
What a cache must not do: hang off the State objects that the Coverer
retains. Coverer.coveredPath keeps every covered step of the round
(X6 of 04-status-MODEL: ~1-2 KB per step), so a children cache stored
in a State would keep every explored subtree of the run alive. The
cache belongs to the planner and is dropped at the start of the next
plan.

F7  What immutability changes, and what it does not
---------------------------------------------------
Changes: the library may drop its per-call String() memo and the
pointer-identity tricks (F1) and say "a State returned by a
StateChange is final; String() is called often, make it cheap". It
may document a caching Walkable (the modeler's de-duplication) as
safe. Nothing in test90 must change for it (F2).
Does not change: the number of paths. With b enabled actions and
depth d the search evaluates b^d leaves whatever the state
representation; de-duplication and precomputed strings reduce the
constant per leaf (F3: today ~5 us) and nothing else. Depth 4 at
b=31 is ~1 M leaves: at 1 us per leaf it fits a 10 s budget, at 5 us
it does so only sometimes. P4 (04-status-MODEL 8) found depth 4 worth
+5% faults in context for 25x the time of depth 3.
