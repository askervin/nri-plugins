Summary
-------
M8 is an error of the SIMULATED SUT (sim.go), not of the model: the
simulator's truth never ended the scheduler's hold of a device (A19),
and it promoted a stalled contender through a decoder hole. The model's
prediction (Blocked) was the right one. Fixing the simulator uncovered
three MODEL errors that the never-ending hold had masked (M9a-c; all
reachable on the cluster, two of them as false failures, one as a
missed check); fixed too, with contract amendment A21 (below). No
driver bug. After the fixes: 4 x 600 steps at depth 3, 1 x 1000 at
depth 2 (the requested runs) and 95 x 1000 steps at depth 2 (seeds
2-96) without a disagreement; the unchanged model on the same 95
seeds: 75 disagree.

3. Root cause, which side
-------------------------
SIMULATOR (M/sim.go resolve, the plain simulator of the experiments
and protocol tests; simref.go, the pool reference of TestRefPoolFuzz,
was right):
a. Held is set by the model's settle and cleared only by the oracle
   (oracle.go resolve, heldSeconds) and by adoption records
   (adopt.go held=DEV:off). The simulator's truth is advanced by the
   model's own transitions and never through the oracle, so its hold
   never ended: every later create of the device in the truth stalled,
   while the model had started it. Real: the hold ends within 5 s of
   the delete, at kube-scheduler's next PreBind poll (bug-08,
   08-analysis section 6, A19 "hold 0-5 s after the delete").
b. resolve promoted a Stalled contender with its controller up to
   Running and pushed its region with no check of a hole. Real (A9,
   bug-02): its claim is allocated and bound, its Prepare needs a new
   region through the hole and fails: Blocked. simref.go (resolveRef)
   and the oracle's resolve (TestReviewF1F3F6StalledPromoted, F3) did
   exactly that already.
c. (same place, latent, the same class) an "any" stalled contender was
   promoted with no node yet: userRegion failed, no region was pushed,
   and the node was drawn afterwards: Running on a node whose stack
   lacks its region. The oracle (node from the pod, region on top)
   then waits for "stack vmX/hbB: committed decoders [], want
   [c-pooled0]" (seen in the base soak, seed 15).
Both (a) and (b) are needed for the exact M8 message; each alone gives
a disagreement of its own (TestReviewM8SimHoldAndHole shows each part
failing without its fix).
Why the cluster did not show it: the real scheduler ends its hold, and
the real kernel refuses the region; only the simulator did neither.

4. The fix of the simulator (commit 2c7f9ca5d, M/sim.go resolve)
---------------------------------------------------------------
- The hold lasts through the window after the settle that ended the
  delete's window, then ends (the step of that settle kept in the
  truth's HeldAt, as the oracle keeps its clock there). That is never
  later than the oracle (which ends it at a settle >= 6 s after that
  one), so a contender that the model starts is never stalled in the
  truth; earlier is legitimate (the model's stall accepts the
  promotion). A real SUT may do either (0-5 s).
- A promoted stalled contender: an "any" one is bound to a host of its
  pool first; its region is pushed unless the stack has a hole, then it
  is Blocked.
No change of the rng draws except where the old code was wrong (the
node draw of a promoted "any" contender moved up), so runs that did
not reach a hold are unchanged: seed 1 depth 3 prints the baseline
@100/@200 numbers.

5. M9: model errors found behind M8 (commit 8c4f663e3, M/actions.go)
---------------------------------------------------------
With the simulator's hold ending in time, the simulator starts
contenders that the model (holding up to 6 s of wall clock longer)
stalls; the model accepts the promotion, but three of its rules about
a stall were wrong. A depth-2 soak (32 seeds x 1000 steps, 12 s each)
found them; the base model on the same seeds failed earlier on M8.
M9a (seeds 3 9 15 16 30) data of a stalled numa contender:
  read xdax-c@any#0: read "junk-94", want "mbt-354"
  (seed 3: 549 create xnuma-c@vm1#0 while c-pooled0 is held: stalled
  in the model, started in the simulator; 552 promoted (adopted
  Running); 563 create xdax-c@any#0 waits, 566 delete xnuma-c@vm1#0,
  568 settle, 569 read.) start() makes a numa claim's device bytes
  unknown (onlined as system RAM, R10); stall() did not, and the
  promotion is adopted from the observation without the effect. Real:
  a promoted numa contender onlines its device: the bytes are gone; the
  model would fail a correct read on the cluster. Fix (actions.go
  stall): a stalled xnuma contender makes its device's data unknown
  (its promotion may come at any time; unknown accepts any read and is
  learnt on-line).
M9b (seeds 2 and 26) a contender stalled only by the hold, racing with
  another share's release:
  seed 26: 624 delete sro-b@vm0#0 (the last share of b-shared0 on vm0,
  stack [hole,b-shared0]), 625 create sdax-b@vm0#0 (fits; held in the
  model: stalled), 626 ctrl-kill b, 627 settle. The simulator, whose
  hold had ended, allocated sdax-b at once and prepared it before
  sro-b's Unprepare: the region stays, [hole,b-shared0], sdax-b Running.
  The model: [] (sro-b's release popped the region and the hole), the
  promotion adopted on top: [b-shared0]: "stack vm0/hb0: committed
  decoders [hole,b-shared0], want [b-shared0]". Seed 2: the same with
  c-shared0 and the other order: Running where the model wanted the
  promotion blocked by the hole the release left.
  The oracle adopts a promotion after every event of the window (on
  top of the settled stack). stallStacksQuiet refuses other region
  events on the stacks of a newly stalled contender but let the events
  of its own device through, because they are what a waiting
  contender waits for (handover). A contender that fits now (stalled
  only by the hold) waits for nothing: it may be allocated at any
  moment of its window (the real hold is 0-5 s after the delete), also
  before the release of another share. Fix (actions.go
  stallStacksQuiet): for such a contender only the prepares of its
  device pass (shares make the region once in any order), a release
  of it is refused like any other event. The planner then does not
  generate that window (A21).
M9c (seed 33) a handover prepare deferred to ctrl-start lost its order:
  653-656 ... ctrl-kill a; 657 delete sdax-a@vm2#0 (the last share of
  a-shared0 on vm2, [hole,a-shared0,local]) promotes the waiter
  sexcl-a@vm2#0 (held in the model: stalled; not in the simulator:
  starting, no prepare with its controller down); 658 ctrl-start a adds
  its prepare with no handover mark; 660 settle: the simulator's truth
  (the model's own transitions) had sexcl-a's prepare BEFORE the
  share's Unprepare: the exclusive claim shared the departing share's
  region, [hole,a-shared0,local], Running. Impossible: the scheduler
  allocated sexcl-a after the share's claim was deallocated, which is
  after its Unprepare; a controller down delays the attach only
  (R1 of 05-review). On the cluster this was a missed check (the model
  accepted an outcome that cannot happen); with the simulator it
  became a disagreement. Fix (actions.go ctrlStart, new
  handoverAfterRelease): a prepare that ctrl-start adds for a pod of
  its pool is a handover when a claim it could not share the device
  with (anything but two shares) was released earlier in the window.

6. Contract amendment (for 04-contract.txt, by the orchestrator)
----------------------------------------------------------------
A21 (20-mbtopt DEBUG, M9) refinements of A19 and A9:
   - A contender stalled by the scheduler's hold may be promoted at
     any moment of its window: a numa contender's device bytes are
     unknown from the stall on (its Prepare onlines the device; R10).
   - A window in which a contender that fits at once (stalled only by
     the hold) shares a decoder stack with the release of another
     claim of its device is not generated: the promotion before or
     after that release gives different stacks, and the oracle adopts
     a promotion only after the window's events. A contender that
     waits for the release of its device still gets it after that
     release (the handover of R1), as before.
   - A pod that got its pool device from a claim released in its window
     prepares after that claim's Unprepare also when its controller
     was down and the prepare comes with ctrl-start (R1).
For 04-status-MODEL.txt section 4: R10 "unknown after a numa use or a
stalled numa contender"; R15 generation rules: "a contender stalled
only by the scheduler's hold: no release of its device on its stacks
in the window either"; R1/R15 events: "a prepare added at ctrl-start
is a handover after the releases it waited for". Simulator notes
(not contract): the hold of the plain simulator lasts one window after
the settle; promoted contenders are blocked by holes.

9. Open issues
--------------
O1 Experiments are not reproducible across planner speeds. expRun's
   oracle runs on time.Now(): the hold of A19 ends at the first settle
   6 s after the one that set it, so after the first hold of a run
   (seed 1 depth 3: step 259; seed 3: 267) the steps depend on how
   fast the planner is. Stream MODEL's acceptance ("the seeded
   experiments print the baseline coverage numbers") holds for the
   @100/@200 marks of seed 1 (before 259), not necessarily for the
   totals at 300 or other seeds. Proposal (not done, it would change
   the baseline): a virtual clock in expRun (e.g. 2 s per step), with
   --steps-out/--from it would also make the "#now" records
   unnecessary. A replay with --from uses the recorded clock already.
O2 The plain simulator decides the M8/M9 timing classes one way each
   (hold: one window; promotion: 50% per settle); TestRefPoolFuzz's
   reference covers the pool side with its own clock. Neither
   exercises a real kubelet/scheduler order beyond that.
O3 The generation rule of M9b is conservative: a release that leaves
   the region to other shares would not race; refused anyway. And
   on-line the planner now avoids that window instead of predicting
   it (as for the other races it does not enumerate).
