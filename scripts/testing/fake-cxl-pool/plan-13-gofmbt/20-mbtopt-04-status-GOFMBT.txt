The contract (design D1, alternative A1)
----------------------------------------
state.go, State:
  A State is immutable once it is given to gofmbt: returned by a
  StateChange or in a Step of a Walkable, passed to BestPath*,
  NewStep or MarkCovered, or held in a Step. What changes in the
  modeled system is a new State, never a modification of an existing
  one. gofmbt calls String() of the same State any number of times
  and expects the same result every time. Coverage functions of
  states, like CoverStates and CoverStateActions, call it for the
  states of every step that BestPath walks, so String() should be
  cheap: if it is expensive to compute, the State should memoize it.
transition.go, StateChange: must not modify the original state, and the
  state it returns must not be modified afterwards; it may return the
  original state if the transition changes nothing.
cover.go, Coverer: "States are immutable (see State): the Coverer keeps
  the steps marked covered and their states, and coverage functions may
  call String() of the states of every step whenever the Coverer needs
  their strings." MarkCovered: the Coverer keeps the steps, their states
  must not change; if the SUT ended up elsewhere than predicted, mark
  NewStep(step.StartState(), step.Action(), observed) with a new object.
search.go, BestPath: the state coverage functions call String() of the
  states of every walked step, so String() should be cheap (see State).
doc.go: the same in the Models section; the "States must not change
  while BestPath is running ... Between calls ... may be updated in
  place" sentence is gone; the on-line example's execute() "returns a
  new State object" and marks NewStep(start, action, observed) (it
  already did).
Removed: every "between the calls they may change" wording (Coverer,
MarkCovered, BestPath, doc.go). This reverses the F1 disposition of
05-review-GOFMBT.txt on the user's instruction.

D2: Walkable ownership and the caching section (0ccea8c)
-------------------------------------------------------
Checked: search.walk shuffles the StepsFrom result in place
(c.shuffle -> rand.Shuffle on the slice) on randomness levels >= 1;
Walker.yieldPaths passes it to the user's step filter, which may
reorder or overwrite it. Kept as is (cheap). Walkable.StepsFrom now
says: the caller owns the returned slice and may reorder it, so a
caching Walkable returns a copy; steps and states are not modified.
doc.go "# Caching and de-duplication" (generic): gofmbt does not
compare, de-duplicate or cache states; StepsFrom is called for every
state of every walked path and coverage functions for every step of
every path, since coverage depends on the path. Caching and
de-duplication are the model's: with immutable states a model may
intern equal states by a key of the fields its transitions and
coverage functions read, and cache the steps of a state (and its own
coverage computations). Musts: return a copy of the cached slice; give
the cache a memory scope, e.g. one BestPath call, because the Coverer
keeps covered steps and their states (a cache hanging off states, or
never dropped, keeps every explored state alive for the round).
Points to TestCachingWalkable. Plus: Actions are best created once
(NewAction/OnAction and their Transitions) and reused by the
generators: creating an Action formats its name.

A2 decision
-----------
Not adopted. The trigger was "the memoizing toy or the CXL model more
than ~20% slower at depth 2 and 3": the memoizing toy is 0-7% faster at
depth 2 and 3, the CXL model has the same paths and plan times within
noise. A model that does not memoize an expensive String() and uses
CoverStateActions pays (toy +56-68%, player +16%): that is the
documented responsibility of the model (memoize in the state); a
library map[State]string would cost a map insert per walked state for
every model that already memoizes.

Actions created once (to report only)
-------------------------------------
In the memoizing toy, creating the transitions and actions on every
StepsFrom (OnAction -> NewAction -> Sprintf, the Do closures) is most of
the time: StateActions d2 12.2 -> 4.0 ms (-67%), d3 869 -> 306 ms
(-65%); States+Actions d3 1407 -> 893 ms (-37%); ActionCombinations2
d3 1020 -> 438 ms (-57%). A CPU profile of StateActions d2 new-memo:
NewAction 34% cum, the toy's generator 56%. The test90 model already
creates its actions once (M/planner.go newMbtModel). The player example
and the toy still create them per call (unchanged: the player is a
readable example; the toy's -once variant shows the difference).

C2: short coverage keys vs readable strings (BenchmarkCoverageKeysToy)
---------------------------------------------------------------------
Memoizing toy, actions once, CoverSteps(1, fn) returning 2 precomputed
strings per step (the action, the action with the end state's
occupancy), readable ~30 bytes ("C0.C1|create p0 kind3 node1 with 2
pods") or a 3-byte key per distinct readable string (a bijection: the
same paths, checked by the benchmark). Median of 5 runs, ms/op:
                     depth 2    depth 3
  none (no strings)    2.32      170       the walk and the toy model
  short-w1             3.64      274
  long-w1              3.59      287
  short-w3             6.32      513
  long-w3              6.36      513
  long-w3-built       15.5      1141       copies built per call, as M/coverage.go weigh does
At depth 3 (~2 M leaves, ~4 M strings with w1) the coverage map costs
~115 ms = ~29 ns per string, 40% of this cheap toy's plan; 3-byte keys
instead of 30-byte strings save 0-5% of the plan (within noise; zero
with w3). The map's cost is the lookup itself (and the cache misses),
not the hashing of 30 bytes. In the CXL model the map is ~6% of the
plan (mapaccess2_faststr 5.6% in base-growth.prof, countNew 33.8 minus
appendStepStrings 27.9). Recommendation: keep readable strings (C2
rejected for gofmbt; nothing in gofmbt to change anyway: a short string
already is a byte array).

C3: weights as copies vs a weight per coverage function
-------------------------------------------------------
Toy, depth 3: weight 3 as 3 precomputed copies costs 513 vs 287 ms for
weight 1 (+79%: 2 more map lookups per string); built per call like
weigh() 1141 ms (+300%: the string concatenation and slice dominate).
CXL model (base-growth.prof): weigh 8.75% of the plan (building the
copies), plus roughly half of the ~6% map lookups being copies (~3%):
~12% in the baseline. But the 8.75% is the model's string building,
which stream MODEL's precomputed strings (design C1, D5) remove
without any gofmbt change (precompute the weighted copies per
transition, as M/planner_ext.go already does for the C1 names). What
a library weight would save on top of that is the copies' lookups,
~3% of a typical plan: under the 10% bar. Not implemented, not
designed further. For the record, what it would take: a new method
(e.g. CoverStepsWeighted(window, weight, fn)), a weight per stepCover,
weighted sums in markNew/countNew/estimateSlow, and a decision on
Coverage() (count strings or weights) and on a string returned by
functions of different weights; ~40 lines and two semantic questions,
for ~3%. Recommendation: no (design C3 default). C4 (Marshal/Key
interfaces) not touched, as decided.

Limitations
-----------
- Immutability is a contract, not checked: a model that modifies a
  state after giving it to gofmbt gets wrong coverage silently (as
  with the old contract during a call). The doc says it in State,
  StateChange, Coverer, MarkCovered and doc.go.
- Non-memoizing models with expensive String() and state coverage
  functions are slower (CoverStateActions: one String() per walked
  step instead of one per parent state; toy +56-68%, player +16%).
- Timings on this host are noisy (+-10% between identical runs, other
  streams running); the CXL comparison rests on identical paths, the
  library's < 2% share in the profile, and interleaved runs.
- bench_test.go's toyState memo is not goroutine-safe (benchmarks are
  single-goroutine); a model sharing states between goroutines
  memoizes race-free (test90: atomic.Pointer).
