20-mbtopt: results
==================
2026-10-27 (orchestrator). The questions of 02-design.txt (Q1-Q3), the
measurements of the streams (04-status-MODEL, -GOFMBT, -DEBUG) and of
the integration, the decisions, and what was left in test90 and in
gofmbt. Long runs of the result: 20-mbtopt-06-runs-SIM.txt and
20-mbtopt-06-runs-CLUSTER.txt. Host: 256 CPUs; the binary runs with
GOMAXPROCS=2 GOGC=400; "d3" = plan time at search depth 3 on the full
profile, seed 1, first 30 steps (mean / max); "growth" = mean plan time
over 300 steps at depth 3 with the default mix; every number with the
virtual clock --step-time 1s unless said otherwise; the runs of one
table row were taken under the same host load, rows of different
tables were not.

1. Answers
----------
Q1 Immutable states: YES, as a contract of gofmbt (A1 of the design).
   What the mutable-between-calls contract had cost was ~95 lines of
   per-call String() memoization and pointer-identity bookkeeping in
   cover.go (746 -> 651 lines), a two-part rule every reader had to
   learn, and a trap that the review F1 of plan-13 had only moved.
   Removing it changed no result (property and reference tests against
   the stock copy, the player example and test06-fuzz output are
   byte-identical) and no plan time of test90 (gofmbt is under 2% of
   its profile; test90 memoizes String() itself). A model with an
   expensive, unmemoized String() pays per walked step now: the toy
   benchmark with a Fprintf String() and CoverStateActions is 56-68%
   slower, with a memoized toy state 0-7% faster (GOFMBT status). The
   contract says so; that is the modeler's job, as the user argued.
Q2 De-duplication belongs to the modeler: YES. gofmbt cannot shorten
   the walk (coverage is path-dependent) and does not know which fields
   of a state matter; it now documents that caching and de-duplication
   are the model's, that the caller owns StepsFrom's slice (a caching
   Walkable returns a copy; TestCachingWalkable shows the pattern), and
   that a cache needs a scope because the Coverer keeps covered steps.
   The model got a canonical key, an intern table and a per-plan cache
   of transitions and coverage strings (canon.go), switchable with
   --plan-states. Measured: it buys ~30% of the plan time at depth 3
   for ~0.7 GB of RSS, almost nothing at depth 4, and it hurts under a
   tighter memory limit (section 3). The default is OFF.
Q3 Compact encodings: used where they pay and nowhere else. The
   fixed-layout byte key of a state (3 bytes per slot, nibbles for the
   stacks, ~200-300 bytes in all) IS the user's Marshal idea, on the
   model side, and it is what makes the intern table cheap. Short
   coverage keys instead of readable strings gained 0-2% in the model
   and 0-5% in the toy (the map is ~6% of a plan): rejected, the
   strings stay readable. A weight per coverage function in gofmbt
   would save ~15% at most (the w prefixed copies cost ~17% per path);
   not added. No State/Action Marshal interface in gofmbt: a string is
   already a byte array, and the library needs only an identity.
The exponent: nothing changed it. The constant per evaluated path went
from ~5.5 us (baseline) to ~4 us without the cache and ~2.8 us with it;
depth 4 still takes 4-6 s mean and 9-13 s max per plan, and P4
(04-status-MODEL 8) measured its coverage gain as small. test90 keeps
depth 3, the mix C1x3,C2x2,C3 and plan-time 10 s.

2. What made the model faster without memory
--------------------------------------------
Found on the way, kept, no memory cost (RSS 57-77 MB, as before):
- outcomesCut (the decoder-stack outcome enumeration, 36% of the
  baseline profile): comparable structs instead of built strings as
  the keys of its de-duplication and DFS memo: -11..16% per path
  (MODEL's patch, applied by the orchestrator).
- StepsFrom loops over the action table itself instead of gofmbt's
  Model/Transition closures; the forced-settle fast path kept.
- C3 pair strings from a lazily filled table indexed by two action
  indexes; C1, C2 and C4 of a step from one coverage function.
- C2 takes the action from the step, not from the end state's Last.
Result (section 3, row "default"): d3 409 ms / 1.76 s against the
baseline's 561 / 2.40 (-27%), d4 4.37 / 9.43 s against 5.91 / 12.8
(-26%), growth 511-538 ms against 650-702 (-21..23%).

  default (no cache)              seed 1 511 ms / 2.85 s  seed 2 538 / 2.71   RSS 77 MB
  --plan-states 150000            seed 1 310 / 1.87                            RSS 719 MB

Long off-line runs (20-mbtopt-06-runs-SIM.txt, 10 runs at a time beside
the cluster test): depth 3, 3000 steps, seeds 11-16 without the cache
458-658 ms mean per plan (max 3.2-5.3 s), 23-33 min wall, RSS 109-160
MB; seeds 11 and 12 with --plan-states 150000: 225 and 296 ms (max
1.65 / 1.98 s), 11-15 min wall, RSS ~730 MB, and the steps files are
byte-identical to the uncached runs' (the cache changes no choice over
3000 steps either).
Coverage is the same in every row (@100 C1=56 C2=120 C3=144 C4=78,
@200 C1=122 C2=191 C3=221 C4=143 for seed 1; the steps taken are
identical: the cache and the strings change no choice).
Reading: the cache is worth ~30% of the plan time at depth 3 when it
may hold its ~300 MB live set under GOGC=400 (RSS ~0.75 GB); below
that the GC eats the gain and worse (the 384 MiB row), and at depth 4
the trees exceed any affordable bound. In the on-line test the plans
are mostly hidden behind the steps (L8: bash waited 10.5 of 120 min
for plans of 0.7-1.2 s): halving the plan time would buy a few per
cent more steps per run. A test generator should not spend 0.7 GB
unasked for that: the default is --plan-states 0; MBT_ARGS="--plan-
states 150000" (and GOMEMLIMIT if 768 MiB is too much or too little)
turns it on for a run or an experiment. The code stays: it is the
modeler's knob, the design the user asked to study, and
TestCanonicalKeyComplete keeps the key honest when the model changes
(a field read by next() or a coverage function that the key omits
fails it, provided the random trees reach states that differ in it).

4. Correctness checks that held
-------------------------------
- Every stage and the final binary take exactly the steps of the
  unchanged model on the seeded runs (growth marks, verb counts, paths
  per depth), once the experiment got a clock that does not depend on
  the plan time (--step-time; without it, the oracle's 6 s hold of a
  device (A19) made even two runs of the same binary diverge after the
  first hold, ~step 260 of seed 1: 04-status-MODEL, 04-status-DEBUG
  O1). The baseline numbers of 01-findings F3 past step 200 belong to
  one particular timing.
- The model's test suite (85 tests incl. canon_test.go,
  covstrings_test.go, the M8/M9 regression tests) passes with the
  main checkout's gofmbt and the stock build (-tags gofmbtstock)
  compiles.
- gofmbt: gofmt, vet, go test, -race, -seeds 1500; player and
  test06-fuzz byte-identical; exported API identical.
- The DEBUG stream's soak of the integrated rules: depth 2, 95 seeds x
  1000 steps, no disagreement (75 of 95 before the M8/M9 fixes).
- L10 (20-mbtopt-06-runs-CLUSTER.txt): test90 for 2 h in the cluster,
  seed 10, defaults (no cache), driver image 392e3abf548d: PASS, 2755
  steps in 2h0m42s with a clean teardown, no oracle difference, no
  panic; coverage 3447 in round 1 (L8 3403, L9 3558). Planning 22.7
  min = 0.48 s/plan (L8 34 min = 0.75, L9 33 = 0.66; -31..36%), bash
  waited 4.75 min (L8 10.5, L9 9.9), longest wait 1.89 s (L8 3.8 s);
  2645 of 2813 plans reused, 84 replanned after an observation that
  differed from the prediction. Settles are 78 of the 120 min, so the
  planning saved buys only a few per cent more steps, as predicted.
- RUNS-SIM (20-mbtopt-06-runs-SIM.txt): 57 off-line runs of the final
  binary, every one PASS, no disagreement: depth 3 x 6 seeds x 3000
  steps (C1 saturates at 215/215), depth 4 x 2 seeds x 400 steps,
  depth 2 x 40 seeds x 2000 steps, the mix "all" x 2 x 1000, the small
  profile on-line and off-line. Recorded runs replay with --from in
  0.04-0.23 s. Note: at depth 4 without the cache the longest plan of
  each seed hit the experiment's 60 s --plan-time, so a fresh depth-4
  run depends on the planner's speed even with the virtual clock;
  raise --plan-time (or turn the cache on) for reproducible depth-4
  experiments.

5. M8 and M9 (found by the baseline measurement, fixed)
-------------------------------------------------------
The baseline growth run stopped at step 330 on a disagreement between
the oracle and the simulated SUT. The simulator was wrong (its
scheduler hold never ended; it promoted a stalled contender through a
decoder hole), and once fixed it exposed three model errors (M9a-c)
that the hold had hidden; all three can happen on the cluster:
amendment A21 (04-contract.txt), three regression tests, and the
experiment got --steps-out/--from so that a 3-minute failing run
replays in 0.05 s. Details: 20-mbtopt-04-status-DEBUG.txt.

6. Decisions (final; D1-D7 of 02-design.txt)
--------------------------------------------
D1 kept: immutable states, no library memo (A1); A2 not needed.
D2 kept: StepsFrom ownership, the doc section on caching.
D3 kept: no Key/Marshal interfaces, no de-duplication, no weights in
   gofmbt.
D4 changed: the canonical key, intern table and per-plan cache exist
   (canon.go) but are OFF by default (--plan-states 0); the memory-
   free parts of the work (section 2) are on.
D5 changed: precomputed C3 pairs and the merged coverage function are
   on; per-transition C1/C2 caching comes with the cache; short keys
   rejected.
D6 met, with the virtual clock added to the protocol.
D7 kept: depth 3, C1x3,C2x2,C3, plan-time 10 s, plan-ahead on.
