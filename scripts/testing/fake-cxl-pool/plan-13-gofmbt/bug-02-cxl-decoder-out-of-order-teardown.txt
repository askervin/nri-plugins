bug-02: a CXL region released out of order blocks every new region
through its host bridge and switch until the regions above it are gone
======================================================================
2026-10-24, debugging agent of plan-13 (from EXEC's S1 and S2,
04-status-EXEC.txt section 9). N = ~/github.com/containers/nri-plugins,
P13 = N/scripts/testing/fake-cxl-pool/plan-13-gofmbt, evidence in
/tmp/bug02.

1. Summary
----------
Linux allocates the HDM decoders of a CXL port (host bridge, switch) in
commit order, as a stack: a new region gets decoder commit_end+1, and
only if every decoder below it is enabled. Destroying a region whose
decoders are not the highest committed ones of a port (allowed since
v6.12) resets the hardware registers but leaves a hole in that stack.
The kernel then refuses EVERY new region through that port, for any
memdev under it and at any address, with EBUSY ("cxl region:
create_region: regionN: failed to set target0 to memM", kernel
"cxl_port endpointK: failed to attach decoderK.0 to regionN: -16"),
until every region above the hole is destroyed; then it reaps the hole
by itself and the port works again. It is not permanent, not decoder
exhaustion, not a stale target of the downstream port and not a qemu
artefact.

In run 2 of test90 the regions on VM0's host bridge 0 were the local
device (decoder 2.0), b-pooled0 (2.1) and b-shared0 (2.2). Deleting the
b-pooled0 pod destroyed the middle region; the next b-pooled0 claim was
refused at every Prepare as long as the b-shared0 pods kept region3.

S2 ("port decoders stay programmed after every run, vm0 hb0 has one
free decoder left") is a misreading, see bug-03: the start and size of
host bridge and switch decoders in sysfs are bookkeeping the kernel
never clears at region teardown. Their decoders_committed was 0 and
their registers were zero in every VM; all decoders were free.

Classification: kernel limitation (design choice of drivers/cxl, not a
bug that a one-line fix removes); the driver tears down in the right
order; qemu commits and uncommits whatever it is told. Real hardware
behaves the same, because the rule is in Linux.

4. Root cause (Linux v7.3-rc4, 93f51579e7df, the VMs' 7.3.0-rc4 build
   of 2026-10-19; qemu ff076b1e6c, branch 5jL-fix-cxl-hot-remove)
------------------------------------------------------------------
drivers/cxl/core/hdm.c
  800 cxl_decoder_commit: commits only decoder number
      decoders_committed (810-815 "out of order commit" -EBUSY), then
      commit_end++.
  889 cxl_decoder_reset: if the decoder is the highest committed one,
      cxl_port_commit_reap; otherwise only dev_dbg "out of order reset"
      (903-908) and commit_end stays. Either way it clears COMMIT,
      zeroes base and size (910-918) and clears CXL_DECODER_F_ENABLE.
  852-887 commit_reap, cxl_port_commit_reap: when the highest decoder is
      reset, commit_end walks down over every disabled decoder below it.
drivers/cxl/core/region.c
  874 check_commit_order, 888 match_free_decoder: the only candidate is
      decoder commit_end+1, and it is refused if any decoder below it is
      not enabled ("unable to allocate %s due to out of order shutdown",
      dev_dbg: this kernel has no CONFIG_DYNAMIC_DEBUG, nor tracing).
  967 cxl_port_pick_region_decoder -> 1198 cxl_port_attach_region:
      "no decoder available" (1243), -EBUSY. Ports are attached from the
      endpoint up, so the switch (port3) refuses first.
  1016-1040 alloc_region_ref: a second barrier, "HPA order violation"
      -EBUSY for a region below one already on the port. The HPA
      allocator is first fit ascending (kernel/resource.c 2140
      alloc_free_mem_region), so a region that fits the hole is put
      there and would be refused by this check even without the first.
  2393 attach_target: "failed to attach %s to %s: %d", the kernel line.
  The cxl tool reports it as "failed to set target0 to memM".
History: 101c268bd2f3 "cxl/port: Fix use-after-free, permit out-of-order
decoder shutdown" (v6.12-rc6, Cc stable): "the CXL specification places
no mandate on in-order shutdown of switch-decoders, the driver enforces
in-order allocation, and hardware enforces in-order commit"; commit_end
"only decrements from 2 after 2 has been disabled". 105b6235ad0f
"cxl/port: Prevent out-of-order decoder allocation" (same series):
allocation order = commit order, no allocation while a reservation is
not committed. Documentation/ABI/testing/sysfs-bus-cxl, regionZ/commit:
"validates that the decoders are being committed in spec mandated order
(last committed decoder id + 1)"; region.c auto_order_ok cites CXL 3.1
8.2.4.20.12 "software must commit decoders in HPA order". (The spec
text itself was not available here: whether hardware may re-commit a
middle decoder whose range fits between its neighbours is the open
question for a kernel change, section 8.)
qemu: hw/cxl/cxl-component-utils.c 118 dumb_hdm_handler (host bridge,
switch) sets COMMITTED on COMMIT and clears it otherwise, with no
ordering or range checks; hw/mem/cxl_type3.c 420 hdm_decoder_commit
("TODO: Sanity checks"). The failing create never reaches a register:
the EBUSY is the kernel's bookkeeping, not the emulated hardware.
The driver: pkg/cxl/memctl ReleaseRegion (memctl.go 1103) offlines,
"cxl disable-region", checks no block stayed online, "cxl destroy-
region"; Release (1065) then "cxl disable-memdev"; the pool driver
(pool.go 759 release) and the controller detach afterwards. That is the
order the kernel wants; nothing is left committed. Nothing the driver
could reset: the hole's registers are already zero and Linux has no
userspace control of switch decoders; the pin goes when the decoders
above it are reset.
So the answers to the questions of the task: the commit order (the
in-order allocation cursor), not the stale switch decoder or its target
port (other downstream ports and higher addresses fail too), not
exhaustion (2.3/3.3 were free; after the reap 2.1-2.3 were used again).

5. Classification
-----------------
Kernel limitation, by design since v6.12: out-of-order teardown is
allowed, out-of-order allocation is not, so a port is a LIFO stack of
regions. Not spec-mandated as far as the kernel's own commit message
says for shutdown; the in-order commit rule is the kernel's reading of
CXL 8.2.4.20.12. Not a qemu artefact (qemu checks nothing; real
hardware may check more, which only makes it stricter). Not a driver
bug. A minor kernel bookkeeping bug that made it look worse is bug-03.

6. Impact
---------
Where: only ports with HDM decoders that carry more than one region at
a time. In the e2e VMs that needs hdm_for_passthrough=on (pxb-cxl
without it has one passthrough decoder: one region per host bridge, no
hole possible); every device of a VM's host bridge 0 (pool-a or pool-b,
and the local memdev) shares the host bridge and the switch decoders,
pool-c shares host bridge 1. On real hardware: a host bridge with
several root ports in use, or a switch (a pooling appliance that binds
logical devices to a host through a switch hot-adds them below the same
upstream port, exactly the fake pool's model). Machines with one device
per host bridge are not affected. Regions made once at boot are not
affected; per-claim regions (this driver: one region per pool device
and node, per local device while it is used) are.
Effect: a claim that needs a new region on that host bridge stays in
ContainerCreating (kubelet retries Prepare, ~64-76 s apart in run 2)
until every pod holding a region above the hole is gone. With long-
lived pods on a shared device that can be for ever, for every device
of that host bridge, also local ones.
test90: sequences "create X, create Y (same VM, same host bridge,
both making a new region, X's first), delete X while Y's region lives,
then create anything that makes a new region there". Regions per node:
exclusive pool device = its claim; shared pool device = first to last
claim on that node; local device = first to last claim or lease slice
(numa and dax slices share it), kept while configured shared. faults
(crash, driver/kubelet/containerd restart, ctrl and server faults) do
not create or destroy regions. Recommendation: predict, do not avoid by
default (real deployments hit it), plus an avoid option for long runs:
- the observer reports the ports of each VM: decoders_committed and
  decoder -> region (one more glob in mbt-observe.py "vm"), so the
  oracle need not guess the creation order of regions made in one
  settle;
- predicted: a pod needing a new region through a port with a hole
  stays pending, its event has "released out of order" (driver message
  of section 7); once the blocking regions are gone it becomes Running
  at kubelet's next retry (settle timeout >= kubelet's back-off; seen
  64-76 s, kubelet caps the pod back-off at 5 min);
- MBT_AVOID-style flag: do not generate a create that needs a new
  region on a (vm, host bridge) whose stack has a hole.

7. What was fixed
-----------------
a. Test infrastructure (pool.source.sh, n4-cxl-shared-2):
   pool-vm-decoders VMDIR (new): prints "portN=COUNT" for every port and
   endpoint with decoders_committed > 0, nothing for none. pool-vm-reset
   restarts the VM also when it has committed decoders (with no devices
   and regions there must be none), checks again after the restart and
   fails if any stay, and ends with "pool-vm-reset: NAME has no CXL
   memory devices, regions or committed CXL decoders": every test with a
   no-devices precondition calls pool-vm-reset just before it, so the
   precondition now covers decoders without touching the tests. It does
   NOT restart for decoders that only show a size without a region
   (bug-03): they are free, and every teardown leaves them, so that
   would restart every VM (and the control plane) before every test.
   pool-vm-require is about qemu and kernel capabilities, unchanged.
   Verification:
   - stub test of the branches plus pool-vm-decoders on the real VM0 in
     the hole state ('port2=3 port3=3 endpoint5=1 endpoint7=1'):
     /tmp/bug02/shtest/pool-vm-reset-test.{sh,out}
   - a temporary test (removed) running pool-vm-reset, the no-devices

     state with 4 memdevs: VM2 and VM1 not restarted (no committed

     error: cxl create-region -t ram -d decoder0.0 -m mem2: exit status

8. Proposals (not done)
-----------------------
P1 Kernel (the real fix; report to linux-cxl with section 2.1, which
   needs only qemu with hdm_for_passthrough=on and three type-3
   devices behind one switch): let match_free_decoder reuse the lowest
   disabled decoder below commit_end for a region whose HPA lies
   between the ranges of the enabled decoders around it (the ascending
   HPA allocator already places a fitting region in the hole), relax
   alloc_region_ref's HPA order check to the neighbours of the chosen
   decoder, and let cxl_decoder_commit re-commit such a decoder without
   moving commit_end. Needs the CXL 8.2.4.20.12 commit checks to allow
   committing decoder m while m+1 is committed when Base[m] >=
   limit[m-1] and limit[m] <= Base[m+1]; to be checked in the spec.
P2 Driver, same-node reuse: keep ("park") the region of a released
   exclusive pool device that is not on top of its ports, so that a new
   claim of the same device on the same node reuses it (CreateRegion
   reuses an existing region already). Fixes exactly S1, needs the
   controller to leave the device attached until it is needed elsewhere
   (then the hole comes anyway). Medium change, controller contract.
P3 Controller placement: when a node is cabled to slots of several host
   bridges, put long-lived (shared) and short-lived (exclusive) devices
   on different host bridges, and prefer a host bridge with no live
   regions; the node driver could publish "port blocked" per host
   bridge for it. Reduces, does not remove.
P4 Repair by compaction: destroy and re-create regions above a hole
   that have no users (none in this driver except a local device kept
   while configured shared). Little value.
P5 test90 model and observer as in section 6.
Side note (known, handled): right after a hotplug the memdev's driver
link exists before its endpoint port: create-region then fails with
"libcxl: __sysfs_device_parse: endpoint4: add_dev() failed ... no active
memdevs found" (7 of 8 immediate tries on freshly booted VM0,
/tmp/bug02/race.log). The driver retries once after 3 s
(pool.go isAddDevError, plan-3); fake-cxl-pool-client "guest region
create" does not retry (test helpers that call it right after a hotplug
may need to wait for the endpoint).
