BUG-20: KUBE-SCHEDULER COUNTS THE CONSUMED CAPACITY OF A CLAIM TWICE
WHILE ITS POD WAITS FOR BINDING CONDITIONS

2026-10-29 (debugging agent). Found by run FR1b (15-runs-fencing-
reboot.txt), step 349. Classification: Kubernetes kube-scheduler v1.37
(DynamicResources plugin), upstream; NOT the CXL driver, the pool
controller or the model. Fixed in K's working tree (uncommitted),
deployed on vm2 as kube-scheduler v1.37.1-dra-requeue.3, verified by
an A/B replay.

K/ = ~/github.com/kubernetes/kubernetes, branch 5jr-dra-requeue (HEAD
4de1561b97c, release-1.37 + the bug-08 fix). DR/ = K/pkg/scheduler/
framework/plugins/dynamicresources/. DRA/ = K/staging/src/k8s.io/
dynamic-resource-allocation/structured/. Line numbers of HEAD unless
"new". Upstream master (dd410c4864a, 2026-10-27) has the same code.

========================================================================
1. SUMMARY

A claim of a pod that waits in PreBind for device binding conditions is
counted twice by the scheduler's allocation state: once from the assume
cache, where PreBind (bindClaim) stored the allocation, and once from
the in-flight allocations, from which it is removed only when PreBind
succeeds. Devices are collected in sets, so a dedicated device is not
hurt by this. The consumed capacity of a device that allows multiple
allocations (consumable capacity) is summed: it counts twice.

dnuma (A39) is the first kind whose pod has both a local claim on the
memdev-c100e2e1 of its node (consumable capacity: 2 blocks of 128Mi on
vm0, 256Mi) and a pool claim with binding conditions (c-pooled0). With
pool-c's controller down, dnuma's 128Mi block counted as 256Mi: vm0's
memdev looked full although only one block was used, and ldax@vm0#0
stayed Pending ("cannot allocate all claims") after the lease companion
had released the other block. It would have stayed so until dnuma's
PreBind ended (binding timeout 10 min) or its claim was deallocated.

Severity: medium (upstream). Any cluster with DRAConsumableCapacity and
DRADeviceBindingConditions: while a pod waits for its binding conditions
(attach of a fabric device, up to the binding timeout), every other
device with consumable capacity allocated to the same pod looks fuller
by that pod's share. Pods that fit are not scheduled meanwhile. No
over-allocation (the error is on the safe side).

4. ROOT CAUSE

- DR/dynamicresources.go Reserve:1615 puts the allocation in flight
  (SignalClaimPendingAllocation). PreBind → bindClaim writes it to the
  claim (UpdateStatus) and stores the result in the assume cache
  (AssumeClaimAfterAPICall, :1892, in the deferred function). The
  in-flight entry is removed only by prebindSuccessCleanup (:1904),
  which PreBind runs when it returns success (:1742), i.e. after the
  binding-conditions poll (:1777, every 5 s up to bindingTimeout, 10
  min). bug-08's fix relies on that hold, so it stays.
- The assume cache emits the update to allocatedDevices (K/pkg/
  scheduler/util/assumecache/assume_cache.go:462), which adds the
  claim's consumed capacity (DR/allocateddevices.go:238).
- DR/dra_manager.go GatherAllocatedState starts from allocatedDevices
  (:381-383) and adds every in-flight allocation (:404-419) without
  checking whether the claim is already included: aggregatedCapacity.
  Insert (:418) sums (DRA/schedulerapi/types.go:171, ConsumedCapacity.
  Add). The device sets (allocated.Insert,
  allocatedSharedDeviceIDs.Insert) are idempotent, so only the capacity
  doubles.
- The allocator compares the doubled sum with the device's capacity:
  DRA/internal/incubating/allocator_incubating.go:1259 (the allocator
  in use, "allocator_incubating.go" in the log) and experimental :1500
  CmpRequestOverCapacity with allocatedState.AggregatedCapacity.
ListAllAllocatedDevices (:321-345) has the same overlap, harmless
there (a set). K/pkg/scheduler/framework/plugins/noderesources/
resource_allocation.go:297 also uses GatherAllocatedState.

5. THE FIX (K working tree, 3 files, +101 -2)

- DR/allocateddevices.go: allocatedDevices tracks the UIDs of the claims
  whose devices it includes (claims, inserted in addDevices, deleted in
  removeDevices under the same lock and revision bump as the devices;
  new :106, :245, :293); HasClaim(claimUID) (new :151) reports whether
  a claim is included, valid for the current revision like Get,
  GetSharedDeviceIDs and Capacities.
- DR/dra_manager.go GatherAllocatedState (new :403-412): skip the
  in-flight entry of a claim that allocatedDevices includes. The
  existing revision check at the end (revision1 == Revision()) covers
  the lookups too: if the set changed during the loop, the caller
  retries. Checking the assume cache instead would race: its events
  reach allocatedDevices asynchronously (emitEvents after the unlock),
  so a claim could be allocated in the cache and not yet in the
  snapshot, and would then be counted zero times.
- Not changed: the in-flight life cycle (bug-08 and pod groups need
  it), ListAllAllocatedDevices (sets; the result is the same).
- Left (upstream note): the special claim of extended resources backed
  by DRA is in flight under a temporary UID (DR/
  extendeddynamicresources.go:189) and gets its real UID when bindClaim
  creates it, so the UID match does not dedupe it; with binding
  conditions and consumable capacity it would still count twice. Not
  used here; a full fix would move its in-flight entry to the created
  claim's UID in bindClaim.
Patch: /tmp/kube-scheduler-dra-capacity.patch; commit message
/tmp/kube-scheduler-dra-capacity.commitmsg (section 8); release note
/tmp/kube-scheduler-dra-capacity.releasenote.

8. PROPOSED COMMIT MESSAGE (K, branch 5jr-dra-requeue)

DRA scheduler: count consumed capacity of a claim in PreBind once

While PreBind waits for device binding conditions, the allocation of a
claim is both in the assume cache, where bindClaim stored it after the
status update, and in flight: the in-flight entry is removed only when
PreBind succeeds. GatherAllocatedState adds the in-flight allocations to
what allocatedDevices has observed in the assume cache. Devices end up
in sets, so for them the overlap is harmless, but the consumed capacity
of a device that allows multiple allocations is summed and was counted
twice. Such a device looked fuller than it was until PreBind finished,
up to the binding timeout, and pods whose claims would have fit on it
were rejected with "cannot allocate all claims".

Track which claims allocatedDevices includes and skip their in-flight
entries in GatherAllocatedState. The revision check, which already
guards the result against concurrent changes, also covers the lookup.
