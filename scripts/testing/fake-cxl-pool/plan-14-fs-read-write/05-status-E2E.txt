Requests
--------
R1 (controller, contract 5) A NEW attachment that fails Conflict because
   one of its wants is exclusive fails every want of its key (today's
   rule kept by the contract). Two claims of one node, a reader and a
   writer, allocated in the same reconcile then fail together, and since
   both go back to the scheduler at the same moment with the same backoff
   they can keep meeting: the reader may never start although it could.
   test20 E has exactly that: ro-d (reader) and rw-e (writer) on VM1 after
   VM2's downgrade; the test waits 240 s for ro-d. Proposed: for a new
   attachment whose exclusive attach conflicts, attach non-exclusively for
   the non-exclusive wants (when that does not conflict too) and fail only
   the exclusive wants: the per-want rule of an upgrade. Without it test20
   E may flake; with it ro-d starts in one reconcile.
R2 (broker, contract 6) The companion MemoryConfig of an fs lease without
   --fs-type must have no fsType key (omitempty): test16's
   lease-companion-check asserts the device section exactly {mode:
   fs, readOnly: true, access}. If the broker writes the detected type or
   "", test16 changes (tell E2E).
R3 (broker/API) "cxl-request list" entries and request responses of fs
   leases carry fsType (api.Response.FsType): test16, test20, test21
   assert it.
R4 (contract 6/7, DOC) resolved by A2: test21 D leases one share
   (--shared) next to the running claims. DOC: leases.md still lists
   "--shared with ... a local memdev that is not configured shared" as
   "not shareable"; with A2 that is a volatile one only.
R5 (info for DRV-POOL) test90's fs readers on a device without a
